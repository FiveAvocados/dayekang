COVID-19: Exploratory Data Analysis

This notebook explores the early spread of COVID-19 using global daily reports from January through April 2020. We inspect the raw observations, clean geographic labels, summarize confirmed cases and deaths, and visualize how the outbreak moved from a local event to a global pandemic.

  • Unit of analysis: country or reporting region by observation date
  • Primary measures: confirmed, deaths, recovered, and active cases
  • Snapshot used for the final map: April 14, 2020

3. Clean and aggregate observations

Standardize country names, fill numeric gaps with zero, calculate active cases, and aggregate province-level rows into one record per country and date.

In [6]:
country_names = {
    "Mainland China": "China",
    "US": "United States",
    "UK": "United Kingdom",
    "Korea, South": "South Korea"
}

cases["Country"] = cases["Country/Region"].replace(country_names)
metrics = ["Confirmed", "Deaths", "Recovered"]
cases[metrics] = cases[metrics].fillna(0)
cases["Active"] = cases["Confirmed"] - cases["Deaths"] - cases["Recovered"]

daily_country = (
    cases.groupby(["ObservationDate", "Country"], as_index=False)
         [metrics + ["Active"]].sum()
)

7. Summary of observations

What the exploratory analysis shows

Early concentration: January reports are dominated by China and nearby countries.

Rapid geographic expansion: During March, confirmed cases accelerate across Italy, Spain, Germany, France, and the United States.

Changing epicenter: By mid-April, the United States reports the largest cumulative number of confirmed cases in this dataset.

Analytical caution: Reported case totals reflect differences in testing, reporting practices, and update timing; they should not be interpreted as a direct measure of underlying infection prevalence.
