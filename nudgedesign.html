Nudge Design to Increase Physical Activities of Hospitalized Children

Footprints nudge children to walk, and their various shapes encourage children to stretch their legs. The story-based wallpaper makes walking more engaging. Children can stretch their necks or arms by following the movements of the animals on the wallpaper.

What inspired this project

I became more interested in problems in children's hospitals while designing a family communication service for children who were hospitalized long term. During the interviews, our team found that a lack of physical activity and play in children's hospitals was a problem. This project was funded by the Samsung Tomorrow Solution.

Service Summary

Reality: Lack of physical activity can cause stress and slow recovery for hospitalized children. However, even though children want to walk, there are no suitable trails that they can enjoy every day.

Problem: Currently, children can use outdoor trails or an indoor hallway for walking. The outdoor trails are easily affected by the weather. Fine dust also prevents children from using them as often as needed. The indoor hallway is dull and boring, so the children do not enjoy walking there. Because a lack of physical activity can slow recovery and cause stress, it is important to ensure that children can take a walk every day.

Solution: By providing a safe and fun trail design, we can help children relieve stress from the daily treatment process. Considering the hospital's budget constraints, we created a nudge design in the hallway to make walking more engaging. Characters and stories on the wall can motivate hospitalized children to take walks every day.

“Why are there no children playing at a children's hospital?”

Importance: This solution can provide a positive hospital experience for children, and it can help them recover more quickly.

Research Process

Desk Research: Online Research

We observed posts on social networks such as Instagram, blogs, and internet cafes to understand physical activity and entertainment in children's hospitals.

Daily life spent in bed all day: The hospital is a shared space, so people avoid letting their children play noisy games. A guardian may give a child a smartphone because the child likes watching videos with a headset, and it is relatively quiet. Due to concerns about spreading infections, it is not easy to meet friends in the hospital.

Field Research: A Quantitative Survey of Hospital Movements

We surveyed thirty-two children for four days, and the survey showed that they moved for no more than 20 minutes in the hospital. According to the paper, children need to walk 12,000–15,000 steps for their physical health, but the hospitalized children did not take more than 2,000 steps.

* About 110 steps per minute for a walk, 140 steps for a fast walk, and 180 steps for jogging.

Observation

We visited children's hospitals, including Asan Medical Center, Severance Hospital, and Seoul National University Hospital, where we observed and interviewed nurses and hospitalized children.

Hey-Jin Kim, 10 years old · Seoul National University Children's Hospital

When we found that she was not bringing all of her things from the refrigerator to her bed at once, we asked her why.

“I intentionally do not bring all the food that I need at once because it forces me to walk more. My doctor always says I have to work out when I'm in the hospital.”

Field Research: Asan Design Innovation Center

We interviewed staff at the Asan Medical Design Innovation Center about children's physical activities. Asan Children's Hospital had no play space for children, and there was little awareness of their physical activity. Regarding children's physical activity, a nurse mentioned that walking was enough: “Since children cannot exercise vigorously in a hospital, walking can be enough.”

“Walking is a great exercise for hospitalized children.”

Affinity Diagram

Based on the affinity diagram, we developed three insights from the stakeholders' viewpoints.

Hospitalized children: They cannot move easily because of IV poles. The space does not feel like a play area because of adult control.

Parents: When children play in the hallway, it is easy to observe their activities and seek medical attention in case of a problem. However, parents were concerned about obstructing the hallway for other patients and clinicians.

Hospital: The hallway is suitable for walking because it does not require extra space. However, nurses are concerned that if children move around without order, there will be problems with medical care and treatment.

Design Insights

“Let's make the hallway a place for the children to play safely”

Nudge Design

Nudge is a concept in behavioral science, political theory, and economics that proposes positive reinforcement and indirect suggestions to influence the behavior and decision-making of groups or individuals.

Nudge design in Amsterdam: red lines suggest that visitors use the stairs rather than the lifts.

Prototype & Testing

Prototype 1: Making a Maze Prototype with Footprints

We used a maze, symbols, and footprints to encourage children to walk and stretch their legs.

Prototype Testing at Kyeongdong Elementary School

We introduced the prototype to the children, who walked on it by following the lines, symbols, and footprints. After the test, we received feedback from the children. They wanted more complex missions and opportunities to play with friends.

Children walking on the prototype

Prototype 1 testing: children gave feedback about the prototype

Prototype 2

We added wallpapers to encourage various stretches. By adding a story to the wallpaper, children can also enjoy the story while they walk. The story is about a bird that gets sick and becomes healthy again with the help of its friends.

Neck stretch: follow the birds trying to fly again! The child can naturally stretch their neck while following the birds in sequence.

Different types of steps: Children can follow the movements of animals and try different types of steps.

Prototype 2 Testing: Usability Test

We tested the prototype with Hyeon-Min (11 years old), and she enjoyed following the footprints. We asked her to carry a suitcase because we wanted to mimic the walking experience with an IV pole. She was able to walk the whole trail without difficulty. When she had a chance to modify the game, she participated actively and suggested different, interesting footsteps.

Outcome & Effects

Hospitalized children: The aesthetically pleasing and playful wall prints can reduce negative images of hospitals. Through physical activity, children can improve their mental health and recover faster.

Parents: They are relieved because the prints act as a guideline. The child can safely play with the design. It may reduce the risk of secondary infections and accidents.

Hospital: The children walk the corridor in an orderly fashion, so there is no disruption to hospital work.
